# Configuration reference

Every feature flag in `.sdlc/config.json` and its default. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Feature flags at a glance

Defaults below are what `/sigma-init` scaffolds (`config.json.tmpl`). Not everything optional ships off — e.g. `discovery.dependency_gate`, `discovery.auto_unpark`, `review.independent`, `handoff`, `work.rebase_upkeep`, `work.enabled` and `work.require_review: "changes"` ship on (`verify.enforce` ships off and turns on when `/sigma-init` records a confirmed verify command), and so do the iteration, minute and token budgets (`max_codex_raw_tokens` ships `0`, off). ## Resource limits

The `resource` block holds the limits `resource_guard.py` derives from the machine. Every key defaults to `null`, which means derived; a bad value (negative, boolean, string, NaN, or a fraction outside 0 to 1) reads as derived with a note. The fractions are policy ratios not yet calibrated against a real exhaustion, so they are labelled uncalibrated wherever printed.

| Key | Default | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| `resource.mode` | derived (`enforce`; also `observe`, `off`) | `observe` computes a hold but never holds; `off` skips the check; uncalibrated |
| env `SIGMA_RESOURCE_MODE` | unset | `off`, `observe` or `enforce` overrides `resource.mode` on every surface (picker, verify, sessions, watcher, doctor, log); unset and no config key resolves to `enforce`; the test suite sets `off`; the doctor shows its resource rows whenever the resolved mode is not `off` |
| `resource.reserve_fraction` | derived (0.20 of total memory) | memory kept free for the machine; uncalibrated |
| `resource.max_share_fraction` | derived (0.60 of total memory) | most memory all sessions together may lease; uncalibrated |
| `resource.cpu_reserve_cores` | derived (one eighth of the cores, at least 1) | cores kept free; uncalibrated |
| `resource.load_hold_ratio` | derived (1.0 per core) | one-minute load per core above which load is reported high; uncalibrated |
| `resource.swapout_hold_mb_per_s` | derived (shown, never holds) | when set, a swap-out rate above it holds and resumes at a quarter of it; uncalibrated |
| `resource.shed` | derived (`verify`; also `off`) | what is shed first under pressure; uncalibrated |
| `resource.shed_after` | derived (3) | consecutive critical samples (2 seconds apart) before the shed stops the verify group; an integer of at least 1, anything else reads as 3 with a note; uncalibrated |
| `resource.max_goals` | derived (no extra cap) | cap on concurrent goals; uncalibrated |
| `resource.max_verify_workers` | derived (no extra cap) | cap on concurrent verify workers; uncalibrated |
| `resource.hold_alert_minutes` | derived (no alert) | minutes a hold may last before it is reported; uncalibrated |
| `discovery.dag.mode` | `"shadow"` | the stored dependency map (`.sdlc/state/dag.json`, schema `dag/1`, codes and numbers only, no titles): `"shadow"` \| `"on"` \| `"off"`. A missing or unrecognised value (including `"OFF"`) reads as `shadow`, the direction that never changes the picker. `"off"` reads nothing, writes nothing and creates no state file. The pick compares the map with the board mirror in the `shadow` default (one numbers-only record per compared candidate in `.sdlc/state/dag-shadow.jsonl`, rotated at 1 MiB, the old decision still returned) and, written exactly `on`, holds from the map when it is usable with a change check in the last ten minutes, else says why on stderr and takes the old path; `dag.py show` prints its mode, age and counts and `dag.py rebuild` reads GitHub (five label lists, no assignee filter, 2,000 per list; a cap hit marks the map partial) and publishes it, printing call counts by class. Writing `"shadow"` or `"on"` explicitly also turns on the automatic refresh (the reconcile tick, the first pick of a process, `dag.py refresh [--force]`), the `dag map` doctor row and the session-start age line (`dag.py line`); leaving the key out starts none of them. A goal Sigma files itself enters the stored map at once (`dag_insert.py`), and a `Blocked by` line that would close a dependency cycle is refused before it is written, naming the members; with no usable map it is checked by a live walk of at most 25 reads, and over that cap the write is allowed and recorded in `state/dag-unchecked.json` (numbers only) until the next saved refresh. Each saved map also appends what changed (an edge added or removed, a node resolved or deleted, a chain completed or superseded; numbers, event names and timestamps only) to `.sdlc/state/dag-history/YYYY-MM.jsonl`, never pruned; `dag.py history --month YYYY-MM` prints one month and the doctor warns above 1 MB in a month |
| `discovery.dag.ttl_minutes` | `60` | a map older than twice this is not used, unless a change check ran within the last ten minutes; the automatic refresh is due after this plus a stable per-clone offset of up to 10 percent, and the doctor row fails after three of these with a live loop or watcher; a bad or non-positive value reads as 60 |
| `discovery.dag.order` | `"chain"` | `"chain"` \| `"off"`. `"chain"` puts the goal that unblocks the most work first on both pick queues (label and board), right after the unit rank and before the bug label: within one priority tier and unit, more open transitive dependents first, then the longer chain of open dependents above the goal (a cycle counts as one step), then the existing key (bug, then oldest). It never crosses a priority tier or the blocking split, and does nothing under `discovery.order: created`. The numbers come from the stored map (`.sdlc/state/dag.json`) under the same gate as the pick-time hold (usable, change check within ten minutes); with `"off"`, no map or an unusable one, the order is exactly the old one. Only `"body"` and timeline edges count, never a comment edge. The label queue sorts the 200 oldest matching issues, so a deeper backlog cannot see a newer chain root until older goals drain |
| `discovery.dag.cycle_comment` | `true` | whether a refresh that saved the map posts one comment per distinct dependency cycle on its lowest-numbered open member (numbers and a fixed instruction only; a marker in the comment stops a repeat). The pick summary, `/sigma-status` and the doctor report cycles either way; mode `off` reports and posts nothing |
| `chain.max_members` | `100` | the largest closure `chain.py plan <sdlc_dir> <N>` plans from the stored dependency map; when the map is off, stale or lacks the goal it reads GitHub live (one REST read per unknown reference) and the bound is the smaller of this and 50. A closure over the bound is refused with its count, never truncated. The verb is read-only toward GitHub and writes only `.sdlc/state/chains/c<N>.json` (issue numbers and labels, no titles or bodies); a bad or non-positive value reads as 100 |
| `chain.auto_longest` | `false` | reserved for offering the longest chain at the start of a pick; nothing reads it yet and the watcher does not honour it. `chain.py plan <sdlc_dir> --longest [--by freeing] [--json]` is the manual gesture today: it reads only the stored dependency map, offers the top three end goals (priority tier, then depth, then member count, then oldest `updated_at`, then number; `--by freeing` puts the member count first), lists any end in or behind a dependency cycle as excluded, and writes nothing and calls no GitHub |

`loop.py verify` goes through admission under the resolved resource mode (`enforce` unless `resource.mode` or `SIGMA_RESOURCE_MODE` says otherwise); `off` runs the proving command exactly as before. Admitted, the command runs in its own process group (a killed caller takes the group with it) under the `verify.timeout_minutes` budget with a 64 KiB output tail. A `{workers}` token in `verify.command` or a goal's `verify_command` is replaced by the granted worker count (an integer only; other braces are left alone), and the child also gets `SIGMA_VERIFY_WORKERS` and `PYTEST_XDIST_AUTO_NUM_WORKERS` set to it. A literal `-n N` cannot be lowered: with a measured cost that N workers do not fit, verify holds instead of running fewer. A hold exits 5 with `RESOURCE-HOLD ... retry_after=<s>` on stderr, runs nothing and writes no evidence, so `record done` still refuses; retry after the stated time. (Exit 5 also still means `UNVERIFIED`, a flaky suite; the stderr prefix tells them apart.) The learned cost is the peak footprint of the verify's process tree divided by the larger of its workers and the number of sizeable processes in that tree (bytes per sizeable process; see the ring unit in [parallelism](parallelism.md#the-resource-guard-derived-limits-holds-shed)), sampled every 2 seconds, so a run shorter than that records its final reading only; a verify admission is also bounded by 1.5 times the largest whole-run peak seen.

## Rulebook rollout notes

`rulebook.rules.auto_approve` (default false, only the JSON boolean true turns it on) lets the watcher draft and approve learned rules as the first configured owner; see [Auto-approve](decision-rulebook.md#auto-approve-opt-in-off-by-default).

The model classifier's spend ceiling, `rulebook.import.max_budget_usd_per_run`, applies to one import run only. There is no daily or cross-run cap, so repeated runs add up; size it with that in mind. With `work.require_review: "approval"`, a host or token that cannot answer the approval query (it needs GraphQL) leaves the goal parked rather than merged. Either use an approval the REST API can see, or accept the parking. Nothing in `work.py` changes this.

## Per-category autonomy thresholds

The autonomy ladder reads one global set of thresholds, `rulebook.autonomy.levels`. A
repository can tune them per category with the optional file `.sdlc/categories.json`, a sibling of
`.sdlc/rulebook.json`. The file is opt-in: the template ships none, and with no file (or the autonomy
gate closed) behaviour and output are unchanged.

Schema `sigma/categories@1`: a `schema` string and a `categories` list. Each category has an `id`
(unique), a `title`, the `areas` it covers (the repository `area:*` names, with or without the
`area:` prefix), optional `qkinds` it is limited to, and an optional `levels` block using the same
keys as the global one (`l1_min_answers` to `l4_min_answers`, `l2_agreement` to `l4_agreement`).

Precedence, per threshold key: the global default, then the first category (in file order) whose
`areas` contain the pair's area and whose `qkinds`, when given, contain its kind. A category
overrides only the keys it names; a pair in no category behaves as before.

The categories reader never raises. A missing file, wrong schema
or unreadable JSON reads as no categories; an entry with a missing or duplicate id (the first wins),
an unknown threshold key or a wrong-typed value is dropped on its own. `autonomy.readout` and the
doctor autonomy row show the matched category and the thresholds in force once the file exists.

`skills/sigma-loop/scripts/resource_lease.py` is the lease table over the same limits (`admit`, `release`, `status`, `wait --max N`). Each admitted unit is a lease file in the same state folder as `hold.json` (`SIGMA_RESOURCE_DIR`, else the XDG state home), taken under an `fcntl.flock` lock so two admitters cannot both see the same headroom; the unmaterialised remainder of every lease (its estimate less what it has used so far) is counted against the next admit. A unit's cost is the p90 of its kind's last 32 measured footprints, values above four times the median excluded and logged; with no observation exactly one unit is admitted and measured. A lease is reclaimed when its process is gone or past its ttl. Admission is paused (a hold with a retry time, exit 10), never a process. POSIX only; the picker, verify and the model-session spawn sites call it under the resolved resource mode. Ratios and the 20 percent, 60 percent and 4x figures are uncalibrated.

## Quality metrics

`quality.metrics` is opt-in and absent from a fresh config, so nothing measures anything until you add it.
Each entry is `{name, command, direction, scope}` plus an optional `required`, `timeout_seconds` and `tolerance`
(read only by the merge gate); `quality.memory_floor_fraction`, `quality.env_drop` and
`quality.gate_timeout_seconds` (the merge gate's budget) are optional block-level keys.
`python3 <installed-sigma>/skills/sigma-loop/scripts/quality_delta.py <goal>` reads the block from `.sdlc/config.json` **as
committed at the merge-base**, never the head's file, runs each command once at the merge-base and once at the
head in scratch worktrees, one at a time, and prints base, head, delta and `better`, `same`, `worse` or
`unmeasured`. A command that fails or times out is `unmeasured`, never `same`. The schema, the number grammar,
the command environment, the limits and the recovery lever are in [quality-delta.md](quality-delta.md).
With `gates.quality_ratchet.enabled`, `work.py merge` acts on `required` metrics; that section of the same page
gives the budget, the worst case and the owner-only `work.py quality-waive` verb.
